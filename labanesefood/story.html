Our Storyقصتنا

A Lebanese grill doesn't need white tablecloths. It needs fire, fresh bread, a stack of boxes and someone at the hatch who is glad to see you.

The corner grill

From a Beirut street corner to a silver Airstream

Walk through Beirut, Tripoli or Sidon at lunchtime and you'll follow your nose to a counter with a turning spit, a hot griddle and a queue that never quite ends. That's where Lebanese people eat every day. It's fast, it's generous and it's made right in front of you.

We brought that idea to the Isle of Dogs in a polished aluminium Airstream. It sits on a patch of green at Skylines Village, against old London brick, with strings of warm bulbs overhead and wooden tables laid out for anyone who wants to stay. The menu is painted on the side of the truck, the grill is open to view, and everything leaves the hatch hot.

أهلاً وسهلاً

“Ahlan wa sahlan” means you are among family and the road ahead is easy. It's the first thing you hear at a Lebanese door, and the spirit of every box we hand over.

The badge

Why there's a cedar on our logo

The cedar of Lebanon, أرز لبنان, sits at the centre of the national flag. These trees grew on Mount Lebanon for thousands of years and were prized by Phoenician shipbuilders and ancient kings. To Lebanese people everywhere the cedar means endurance, roots that hold firm whatever the weather.

We refined our original brushed-orange sign into a round badge with the cedar at its heart, set on the deep green of our “Hot & Fresh” banner. It's a small reminder of where the recipes come from, now parked in East London.

Sufra · سفرة

The table is for sharing

In a Lebanese home the meal arrives all at once. Bowls of hummus and garlic yoghurt land in the middle, plates of rice and salad go round, and nobody eats alone. Generosity is the rule, and an empty plate is quickly filled again.

That's why every one of our boxes carries hummus, salad and sauce alongside the grill, and why we set out tables on the green. Bring friends, order a spread and pass it around.

Words from the hatch

A little Lebanese glossary

Order like a regular. Here's what the words on our board mean and how to say them.

Shawarma

شاورما
shah-WAR-ma

Meat stacked and slow-roasted, then shaved thin. The name comes from the Turkish çevirme, “turning”, after the spit that turns all day.

Kofta

كفتة
KOFF-ta

Minced lamb worked with onion, parsley and warm spice, pressed onto skewers and grilled until the outside crackles.

Hummus

حمّص
HOOM-us

In Arabic the word simply means chickpea. Blended with tahini, lemon and garlic, it goes on every box we make.

Falafel

فلافل
fa-LAH-fel

Ground chickpeas, herbs and spice fried into crisp rounds that stay green and soft inside. The vegetarian heart of Levantine street food.

Halloumi

حلوم
ha-LOO-mee

A firm brined cheese that holds its shape on the grill and squeaks between your teeth. Loved across the eastern Mediterranean.

Toum

ثوم
TOOM

Arabic for garlic, and the name of the whipped garlic sauce that Lebanese grills pour over chicken.

Sumac

سماق
SOO-mak

Dried crimson berries ground to a tart, lemony powder. Sprinkled over onions and salads for colour and zing.

Ayran

عيران
eye-RAN

Chilled salted yoghurt drink. It cools the chilli and is the classic partner to a kebab.

Sahtein

صحتين
sah-TAIN

What you say when the food arrives: “two healths”, or enjoy your meal. The reply is ala albak, on your heart.
